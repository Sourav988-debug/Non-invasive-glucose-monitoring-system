# Research Foundation

This project is grounded in research on NIR spectroscopy, PPG-based glucose estimation, physiological signal processing, embedded inference and multi-wavelength optical sensing.

## Primary reference

**Rajeswari, S. V. K. R., and Vijayakumar, P.** “Development of sensor system and data analytic framework for non-invasive blood glucose prediction,” *Scientific Reports*, 14, 9206 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-59744-7.

The article is open access under CC BY 4.0. The repository provides a citation record and official source link for the paper.

The paper describes a 940 nm NIR prototype, finger and wrist sensing, preprocessing/data analytics, Ridge Regression and handling of skin colour, ambient light and sensor pressure.

## Additional references

1. **Zeynali, M., Alipour, K., Tarvirdizadeh, B., et al.** “Non-invasive blood glucose monitoring using PPG signals with various deep learning models and implementation using TinyML,” *Scientific Reports*, 15, 581 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-84265-8. Relevant to PPG-based estimation and embedded/TinyML deployment.

2. **Nguyen, A. T. P., et al.** “The open-source multi-wavelength non-invasive blood glucose sensing system,” *Scientific Reports*, 15, 45731 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-28673-4. Relevant to open-source multi-wavelength optical sensing and transparent validation.

3. **Zhang, Z., Li, J., Wang, Y., Yang, K., and Lei, T.** “Non-invasive Blood Glucose Estimation from Wearable Physiological Signals,” arXiv (2026). Relevant to participant-independent and longitudinal evaluation.

4. **Yao, C., et al.** “Adaptive personalization for wearable non-invasive continuous glucose sensors,” *Nature Communications* (2026). Relevant to individual variability in non-invasive glucose sensing.

## Why these references matter

The literature supports the project's major engineering directions without proving that any particular extension automatically improves glucose estimation. Multi-wavelength sensing, PPG-based modeling and embedded inference are active research areas, while subject-independent evaluation remains important for assessing generalization.
